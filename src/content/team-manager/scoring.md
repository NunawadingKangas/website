## Scoring roster

There are two scorers for each game, one from each team. One scorer operates the iPad and the other operates the scoreboard display.

The Team Manager should prepare a scoring roster so the responsibility is shared across team families during the season.

## Scorebench behaviour

Scorers are assistants to the referees and are considered game officials. Barracking, coaching, or questioning referee decisions from the scorebench is not allowed.

The iPad is the official score record, so the iPad and scoreboard should match throughout the game. If there is a discrepancy, the scorers should identify the issue and ask the referees for help if needed.

## Electronic scoring

Before the game, check active players and player numbers in the scoring system. Add fill-in players if needed.

At the end of the game, it is a good idea for the Team Manager to take a photo of the final scoring screen. Occasionally results do not upload correctly, and the photo gives the team a useful reference.

## Manual scoring basics

If manual scoring is required:

1. Enter player numbers and names in numerical order on the team sheet.
2. Enter the coach's name.
3. Record points beside the relevant player's name and number.
4. Use the running score boxes as points are scored.
5. Record fouls in the boxes beside each player's name and number.
6. Record team fouls in the team foul boxes.
7. Record timeouts by crossing through the appropriate timeout box.

## Free throws

For free throws, draw a box on the player's score line. For each successful free throw, one point is awarded and a line is drawn through the box. Unsuccessful free throws are marked with a dot.

If a player scores and is fouled, record the made basket and circle the bonus free throw. If the bonus free throw is successful, mark it and add the point to the running score.

## Fouls

When a player reaches five personal fouls, sound the siren and tell the referee. That player can take no further part in the game.

When a team reaches the team foul limit in a half, sound the siren and tell the referee. Subsequent fouls may result in free throws depending on the game situation.

## Half time and final checks

At half time, the referee checks and initials the scores and fouls. Keep the score accurate throughout the game because score reversals after the game are rare.

If there is any dispute about the score, call the venue supervisor before the game is closed off.

## Last two minutes

In the last two minutes of the second half, the clock stops for every whistle. The clock restarts when the ball touches a player on the court.

For free throws, the clock restarts when a player touches the ball after the shots, either from a rebound or after the ball is thrown in.
